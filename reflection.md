# AI Use Reflection

I used ChatGPT to help structure the policy brief, extract relevant concepts from the supplied lecture slides, identify search terms for primary sources, improve Markdown formatting and check the logic of the costing assumptions. I also used it to draft and edit sentences. I did not treat its summaries as evidence. I opened the original DEWR, Jobs and Skills Australia, Productivity Commission and government policy sources and checked every statistic and policy claim before retaining it.

One important instance of rejecting AI output concerned the relationship between exposure and job loss. An early formulation implied that slower employment growth in highly exposed occupations demonstrated AI displacement. I rejected that causal wording after checking DEWR's 2026 report. Its core estimate is sensitive to the exposure measure and the decline in routine cognitive work predates ChatGPT. I rewrote the proposal to describe the evidence as an early-warning signal and made the pilot conditional, staged and independently evaluated. I also substantially edited a suggestion for universal training credits because it duplicated the government's existing microskill program and risked paying for training that would occur anyway.

The main limitation was that fluent AI output could hide uncertainty, outdated information or invented precision. This was especially risky because DEWR's report was newly released and the assignment required current policy context. Primary-source verification and transparent costing assumptions were therefore essential. AI accelerated organisation and editing, but I remained responsible for the argument, source selection and final judgements.
