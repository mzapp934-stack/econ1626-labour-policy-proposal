# AI Use Reflection

I used ChatGPT to structure the brief, extract concepts from lecture slides, identify search terms, improve Markdown and check the costing logic. I also used it to draft and edit sentences. I did not treat its summaries as evidence. I opened the original DEWR, Jobs and Skills Australia, Productivity Commission and government policy sources and checked each statistic and policy claim.

I rejected one AI formulation that implied slower employment growth in highly exposed occupations demonstrated AI displacement. DEWR's 2026 estimate is sensitive to the exposure measure, while routine cognitive work was declining before ChatGPT. I rewrote the claim as an early-warning signal and made the pilot conditional and independently evaluated. I also substantially edited a suggestion for universal training credits because it duplicated the existing microskill program and risked subsidising training that would occur anyway.

The main limitation was that fluent AI output could hide uncertainty, outdated information or invented precision. This was especially risky because DEWR's report was newly released. Primary-source verification and transparent assumptions were therefore essential. AI accelerated organisation and editing, but I remained responsible for the argument, sources and final judgements.
